# Neurosymbolic Approach — Findings & Method Outline

> **Scope.** High-level outline of *how* to attack the Infineon "Process Logic"
> track with neurosymbolic AI. This is a methods map, not an implementation
> guide — no architectures-as-code, no hyperparameters. It answers two questions
> the team asked first:
>
> 1. **Are the rules logically incompatible?** → No. They form a clean,
>    conflict-free, decidable theory. (§2)
> 2. **Can we build a "KELM for sequence prediction"?** → Yes, and we're already
>    running a primitive version of it. The upgrade is to verbalize the *rules*
>    and their *violations*, not just sample valid sequences. (§3)
>
> Everything else (§4–§8) is the method menu and how it composes.

---

## 0. TL;DR

- **The symbolic half is handed to us.** Unlike most neurosymbolic problems, we
  do not have to *induce* the logic — the track ships a formal grammar, 10
  ordering rules, and a deterministic O(n) validator (`generate_sequences.py` /
  `src/data/validator.py`). The entire opportunity is to stop *learning* what we
  can *verify*.
- **Division of labour:** *logic owns what is **possible**, learning owns what is
  **probable**.* Symbolic decides validity (Task 3 + pruning the candidate set);
  neural decides preference/likelihood among valid options (ranking for Tasks 1 & 2).
- **The crux is Task 4 (hidden 4th family, OOD).** A pure-neural model memorizes
  family-exclusive step n-grams and falls off a cliff (the existing log shows
  Top-1 0.72 → ~0.48 leave-one-family-out). The rules and the *step roles* are
  family-agnostic, so a neurosymbolic model transfers the part that actually
  generalizes. **Role-level abstraction is the single biggest OOD lever.**
- **Honest-framing caveat:** the validator is the organizers' own code. Using it
  as the Task-3 classifier is a legitimate ~100% submission *and* a ceiling/oracle —
  but the rubric rewards *learned* process understanding, so the report should
  present symbolic-oracle and learned-model side by side, not hide one behind the other.

## 1. The setting in one paragraph

We predict semiconductor process step-sequences (~115–150 steps, vocab of
**198 distinct steps**). Three scored tasks: **(1) next-step prediction**,
**(2) sequence completion**, **(3) anomaly detection**. A fourth, unscored-by-us
task measures **OOD generalization to an undisclosed 4th product family**. The
data is generated by a context-free-ish block grammar and filtered by 10
ordering rules; the same rules define the anomaly labels. The grammar is
*given*, the validator is *given*, the metrics script is *given*. This is the
rare case where the symbolic knowledge base is complete and exact before we
write a line of model code — so the right question is not "can a model learn the
rules?" but "where is learning even necessary, and where does symbolic reasoning
strictly dominate?"

**Quantitative anchors (measured, not assumed):**

| Fact | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Provided sequences valid under all 10 rules | **3000 / 3000** | ran `--validate` on all variant CSVs |
| Distinct steps across 3 families | **198** | counted |
| Steps shared by **all** 3 families | **94** (~48%) | counted |
| Family-exclusive steps | MOSFET 20 · IGBT 27 · IC 29 | counted |
| Valid-sequence combinatorics | MOSFET ~51B · IGBT ~13B+ · IC ~6B | rules doc |
| Pure-neural OOD drop (LoFO) | Top-1 0.72 → ~0.48 | existing `FINDINGS.md` |

The 94/198 split is the whole story for Task 4: **roughly half the surface
vocabulary transfers, the family-exclusive half does not — but the *grammar*,
the *10 rules*, and the *functional role* of every step transfer completely.**

## 2. Question 1 — Are the rules logically incompatible?

**No.** The question has three readings; all three resolve cleanly in our favour.

### 2a. Satisfiability (are the rules mutually consistent?)
**Constructively yes.** The generator is itself a *constructive consistency
proof*: it emits sequences and self-validates, and we confirmed **3000/3000**
provided sequences satisfy all 10 rules simultaneously. A theory with 3000
distinct models is not contradictory. There is no pair of rules that cannot both
hold.

### 2b. Logical form (why a symbolic engine is even viable)
All 10 rules are **guarded precedence/ordering safety constraints** — Horn-like
implications of the form *"if trigger step T occurs, then prerequisite P must
have occurred (within a window / earlier in the trace)."* They fall into exactly
three shapes:

| Shape | Rules | Logical character |
|---|---|---|
| **Windowed existence** ("P within k steps before T") | DEP_NO_CLEAN, METAL_ETCH_NO_LITHO, ETCH_NO_MASK, IMPLANT_NO_MASK, CMP_NO_DEP | bounded-lookback safety; checkable with a sliding window / counter |
| **Monotonic order** ("levels non-decreasing, no skips") | LITHO_LEVEL_SKIP | integer-monotonicity over a projected subsequence |
| **Global precedence** ("T only after anchor A") | PAD_OPEN_BEFORE_DEP, TEST_BEFORE_PASSIVATION, SHIP_BEFORE_TEST, BACKSIDE_BEFORE_PASSIVATION | partial-order "happens-after" constraints |

Consequences:
- The global-precedence rules induce a **DAG** over events (ship after sort;
  tests / backside-metal / pad-open all after `CURE PASSIVATION`; etc.). **There
  is no cycle** (no rule says "X after Y" while another says "Y after X"), so the
  partial order is **linearizable → satisfiable by topological sort**. Three
  rules share the single `CURE PASSIVATION` anchor and are jointly consistent
  (all just require "after cure").
- The windowed rules are **local existence** constraints the grammar satisfies by
  always inserting the prerequisite (a clean before a deposit, a develop before
  an etch). They never fight each other — they constrain disjoint trigger sets
  (deposition vs etch vs implant vs CMP).
- Therefore the rule set is expressible as a conjunction of **LTL-over-finite-traces
  (LTLf)** properties ⇔ a **finite-state safety monitor** ⇔ a small **Datalog/ASP**
  program. All decidable, all O(n) to check. **This is exactly what makes the
  symbolic side exact and cheap.**

### 2c. The asymmetry that matters: grammar ⊊ rules
The grammar is **sound** w.r.t. the validator (everything it generates passes —
3000/3000), but the validator's "valid language" is **strictly larger** than the
grammar's: the 10 rules say nothing about most block ordering, do not require the
PREFIX, do not pin the TEST_SUITE internal order beyond ship-after-sort, etc. So:

> **The anomaly-detection decision boundary is the 10 rules, *not* the grammar.**
> Eval "invalid" sequences are valid sequences with one *injected rule violation*;
> eval "valid" sequences pass all 10 rules by construction. A checker that
> implements exactly the 10 rules therefore sits *on* the true boundary →
> near-perfect precision/recall **and** rule attribution, with essentially zero
> false positives on grammar-clean valids.

### 2d. The one structural subtlety (and it's an OOD lever, not a bug)
Every rule is defined over **step *roles*** (the validator's frozensets:
`DEPOSITION_STEPS`, `ETCH_STEPS`, `CLEAN_STEPS`, …), not free text. This is
*why* the rules are family-agnostic — and *why* the validator has a blind spot:
a 4th-family step string that is semantically a deposition but lexically unknown
(e.g. `DEPOSIT HIGH-K DIELECTRIC`) is invisible to `RULE_DEP_NO_CLEAN`. That is
not an inconsistency; it is the precise seam where a learned **role-induction**
component earns its keep (§4-C, §4 anomaly-residual). Flagged again in §7.

**Verdict:** the rules are consistent, conflict-free, decidable, and
linearizable. They are not just "compatible" — they are about the friendliest
symbolic theory you could hope to wrap a neural model around.

## 3. Question 2 — A "KELM for sequence prediction"?

### What KELM actually is
Google's **KELM** (Agarwal et al., NAACL 2021, *"Knowledge Graph Based Synthetic
Corpus Generation for Knowledge-Enhanced LM Pre-training"*) does three things:
1. Take a **symbolic knowledge base** (the Wikidata knowledge graph — triples).
2. Train a **data-to-text verbalizer** (**TEKGEN**) that turns KG subgraphs into
   fluent natural-language sentences.
3. Run it over the whole KG → the **KELM corpus** (~18M synthetic sentences) →
   pretrain/augment an LM on it so structured knowledge is *baked into the
   weights*.

In neurosymbolic terms this is **Kautz Type-4, `Neuro : Symbolic → Neuro`**:
compile symbolic knowledge into a neural network via a generated corpus.

### The mapping to our problem (it's almost 1:1)

| KELM component | Our analogue |
|---|---|
| Knowledge graph (the KB) | The **block grammar + 10 rules + 12-category step-role ontology** (+ the fab-parameter tables) |
| TEKGEN verbalizer (learned, hard part) | **`generate_sequences.py`** — and it's *exact*, not learned (no distant supervision needed) |
| KELM corpus (verbalized facts) | Synthetic valid sequences **+** verbalized rules **+** counterfactual valid/invalid pairs **+** repair edits |
| Knowledge-enhanced LM | The **process transformer / xLSTM** already in `src/model/` |

### Verdict
**Yes — and you are already running a weak KELM.** "Grammar → generate
sequences → train transformer" *is* synthetic-corpus knowledge injection. KELM's
expensive step (learning a faithful verbalizer) is free for us because we own a
perfect generator. So the interesting move is not to reproduce KELM but to
**extend the corpus beyond positive examples**:

- **Verbalize the rules themselves** — as a natural-language / templated preamble,
  or as special "rule tokens," so the model sees the *constraint*, not only
  conforming data.
- **Counterfactual pairs** — `(valid seq, minimally-corrupted invalid seq, rule
  label)`. This teaches the *decision boundary* directly (powers a learned,
  explainable anomaly detector and hardens next-step prediction). `src/data/corrupt.py`
  already exists to manufacture these.
- **Repair edits** — `(invalid seq → valid seq, edit)`. Teaches corrective
  behaviour; directly improves completion robustness.

This is the honest novelty line for the report: **KELM injected *facts*; we inject
*rules, their boundaries, and their repairs*.** Call it process-logic distillation
into the sequence model. It is the mechanism that should make the neural model
transfer to family-4 by *structure* rather than by *memorized n-grams*.

## 4. The method menu (ordered by ROI)

Framed on the standard integration spectrum (symbolic-around-neural →
tightly-coupled). Each entry: what it is · which task · why it helps OOD · cost.

### A — Symbolic-as-oracle for anomaly (Task 3) · `Neuro ; Symbolic`
The validator **is** the classifier. Run all 10 rules; return `IS_VALID`,
`SCORE`, and the triggered rule as `PREDICTED_RULE`. On known-vocab families this
is ~100% accuracy *and* near-perfect rule-attribution, for free.
- **OOD angle:** generalizes perfectly *to the extent the 4th family's steps are
  recognized* (§2d). Pair with role-induction + a small learned **residual
  detector** that catches violations involving *unknown* step strings and
  gross distributional anomalies.
- **Cost:** ~zero (code exists). **Highest ROI on the board.**

### B — Constraint-guided decoding for Tasks 1 & 2 · `Neuro ; Symbolic`
At each decode step, mask candidates that would introduce a violation; *symbolic
proposes the legal set, neural ranks within it.* The existing log already shows
this works (grammar-trigram: Top-3 +1.3pp; MOSFET completion NED 0.999 → 0.126).
**Extension:** go beyond "don't break the 10 rules" to "stay on the **grammar
manifold**" by decoding against the grammar-compiled automaton (§4-E), which
prunes far more aggressively than the 10 rules alone.
- **OOD angle:** constraints are family-agnostic, so even when the neural model
  is uncertain on family-4, the mask keeps every emitted step legal.
- **Cost:** low; inference-time only; reuses the validator.

### C — Role / type abstraction (two-level tokenization) · structural
**The single biggest OOD lever.** Map every concrete step to its functional
**role** (the 12 categories already enumerated in the rules doc / the validator's
sets). Model the sequence at **two levels**: predict the *role* (highly
transferable — family-4 shares roles) then the *concrete step* conditioned on
role + family. Rules live and are enforced at the **role level**, which is
family-invariant.
- **OOD angle:** turns family-4 from "unseen tokens" into "seen roles, possibly
  new surface forms" → an in-distribution problem at the layer that the rules
  care about. Directly attacks the 94/198 transfer gap.
- **Cost:** medium (tokenizer + head changes); composes with everything else.

### D — KELM-style knowledge injection (train-time) · `Neuro : Symbolic → Neuro`
The §3 corpus upgrade: rules-as-text + counterfactual pairs + repair edits mixed
into pretraining. Bakes constraint semantics into the weights so the model
behaves well even *without* the inference-time symbolic wrapper (useful for the
"learned understanding" narrative and as a fallback when the checker is blind).
- **OOD angle:** model internalizes *why* a step is (in)valid, not just which
  bigrams co-occur. Cost: medium (data generation, already half-built).

### E — Grammar/rules → automaton compilation · symbolic infra that powers B & "metrics"
Compile grammar + rules into a **DFA/pushdown monitor**. Gives three things:
(i) exact constrained decoding (B); (ii) a "valid next-set" oracle for next-step
candidate generation (Task 1 Top-k/MRR); (iii) a principled **distance-to-language**
diagnostic — how far is a model's free output from the valid language? Reframes
the whole task as *"learn the residual preference distribution over an
automaton-defined support."*
- **Cost:** medium-high one-time; high leverage, very strong report material.

### F — Symbolic repair / projection post-processor · `Neuro ; Symbolic`
Project any neural completion onto the **nearest rule-valid sequence** (minimal
edits to satisfy the monitor). Guarantees a valid Task-2 submission even if the
neural beam drifts; cheap insurance on edit-distance / block-accuracy.
- **Cost:** low–medium; inference-time only.

### G — Logic-as-loss / tight coupling (stretch) · `NeuroSymbolic` (Type-5)
Add a differentiable penalty (semantic loss / DL2-style, or posterior-regularization
teacher–student à la Hu et al. 2016) that punishes probability mass on
rule-violating continuations during training. Deepest integration; likely
overkill for the time budget but the natural "what we'd do next" stretch and a
strong scaling/architecture-comparison story.
- **Cost:** high. List as stretch, not critical path.

## 5. How it composes — one system

```
                          ┌──────────────────  SYMBOLIC CORE  ──────────────────┐
                          │  grammar  ·  10 rules  ·  role ontology  ·  params   │
                          └───────────────┬───────────────────────┬─────────────┘
                                          │ (D) KELM corpus:       │ (E) compile
                                          │  seqs + rules +         │  → automaton /
                                          │  counterfactuals +      │     monitor
                                          ▼  repairs                ▼
        TRAIN TIME                ┌─────────────────┐        INFERENCE TIME
        ─────────                 │  NEURAL CORE    │        ────────────────
        role-factored (C) ──────► │  transformer /  │ ─────► (B) constrained decode
        logic-as-loss (G) ──────► │  xLSTM + heads  │        (F) repair / projection
                                  └─────────────────┘
                                          │
            ┌─────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┐
            ▼                             ▼                                ▼
      Task 1 next-step            Task 2 completion                Task 3 anomaly
   symbolic valid-set (E)      constrained beam (B)             (A) validator oracle
   + neural rank (C)           + repair (F)                     + role-induction +
                                                                learned residual detector
```

**Who owns what:**
- **Task 3** → symbolic oracle (A) + residual learner for OOD blind spot.
- **Task 1** → automaton valid-set (E) as candidates, neural (C) ranks → Top-k/MRR.
- **Task 2** → constrained beam (B) + symbolic repair (F).
- **Task 4** → role abstraction (C) + KELM injection (D) carry the neural side;
  the symbolic side (A/B/E/F) is family-agnostic by construction.

## 6. Why this beats pure-neural *specifically on Task 4*

A pure neural model's Task-4 loss comes from the **20–29 family-exclusive steps**
it has never seen and the family-specific n-grams around them — the LoFO drop
(0.72 → ~0.48) is exactly this. Neurosymbolic attacks every term of that loss:

- **Validity never degrades** — rules are family-independent; constrained
  decoding and the anomaly oracle hold at ~full strength on family-4.
- **Roles transfer** — the 4th family is built from the *same 12 functional
  roles* and the *same shared backbone* (logistics → clean → prep → cycles →
  ILD/via/metal → passivation → backside → test → ship). Predicting at the role
  level is near-in-distribution even when surface tokens are new.
- **Structure over memorization** — KELM-style rule/counterfactual injection
  teaches the *why*, which is the part that generalizes.

Expected shape of the result for the report: pure-neural shows a large ID→OOD
drop; the neurosymbolic stack shows a **much flatter** drop, and the gap *is* the
contribution. (The symbolic components define a near-flat ceiling; the
interesting science is how close the *learned* model gets to it OOD.)

## 7. Risks & what to verify next

1. **4th-family vocabulary coverage (the one real risk).** If family-4 introduces
   deposition/etch/clean steps under new strings, the validator's frozensets miss
   them. *Mitigation:* role-induction by lexical/semantic pattern (`DEPOSIT *`,
   `* ETCH`, `* CLEAN` / `CLEAN *`, `IMPLANT *`, …) to map unknown steps onto
   roles before checking — a small, testable neurosymbolic layer. **Verify** by
   simulating an OOD family (a `generate_ood_families.py` already exists) and
   measuring oracle recall on unknown-vocab violations.
2. **Are eval "valid" sequences guaranteed validator-clean?** If yes, symbolic
   false-positive rate ≈ 0. If the organizers' valids come from a different
   generator, check for spurious violations. **Verify** against any released
   sample of the eval format.
3. **Rule-attribution ambiguity.** An injected violation can incidentally trigger
   a second rule, or the checker may name a different rule than the label.
   Attribution is scored by exact string match, so prefer returning the *minimal*
   / first-triggered rule. **Verify** by round-tripping `corrupt.py` outputs
   through the validator.
4. **"In the spirit?" / rubric alignment.** Using the organizers' validator at
   inference is explicitly enabled (they ship it; the existing plan already uses
   it in decoding). But the rubric rewards *learned* understanding — so frame the
   oracle as **baseline/ceiling** and report the *learned* model's standalone
   numbers next to it. Do not let a 100% symbolic Task-3 score hide the absence of
   a learned story.

## 8. Relationship to the existing (pure-neural) plan

This is **complementary, not a replacement** — it wraps the work already in the
repo rather than restarting:

- **Reuse:** `src/data/validator.py` (rules), `tokenizer.py` (extend to roles for C),
  `corrupt.py` (counterfactuals for D), `physics.py` (parameter features for
  role-induction / OOD), the trained transformer & xLSTM checkpoints, and the
  existing grammar-constrained decoder (the seed of B).
- **The "2-to-tune, 3-to-ship" protocol still holds.** Use leave-one-family-out to
  measure whether each neurosymbolic component *flattens* the OOD drop, then retrain
  the winning recipe on all 3 families to ship. The symbolic components don't need
  the held-out family to generalize — that's the point — so LoFO cleanly isolates
  the *neural* generalization gain from each piece.
- **Compute (4× A100):** symbolic components are CPU/inference-cheap; GPU budget
  goes to (C) role-factored training and (D) KELM-corpus pretraining, not to
  bigger models (the log already shows ID loss is flat past ~5M params).
